# Brand Foundation — Fernando Bittencourt

## Identity

Fernando is presented as an individual creator with the sensibility of an experimental creative studio. The portfolio is personally authored; it is not disguised as an agency or fictional company.

The public display name may use **Fer Bittencourt**. Fernando Bittencourt remains the full personal name for metadata, accessibility, and contexts where clarity matters.

## Intended perception

Visitors should perceive Fernando as:

- Current and attentive to contemporary culture.
- Artistic without sacrificing clarity.
- Technically capable and curious.
- Comfortable connecting creative thinking, frontend development, and marketing.
- Actively developing toward AI, business automation, and data analytics.

## Voice

- Primary language: English.
- Direct and conversational for essential information.
- Poetic and enigmatic in carefully chosen moments.
- Curious, thoughtful, and personally authored.
- Honest about current experience and future direction.

Avoid corporate jargon, inflated expertise, generic superlatives, and writing so cryptic that the visitor cannot understand Fernando's capabilities or intention.

## Personal presence

Fernando's identity should come through his name, ideas, writing, decisions, process, and work—not through personal photography.

The absence of a portrait should create authored mystery, not anonymity. Contact information and first-person language must remain clear.

## Cultural raw material

These are discovery inputs, not a finalized visual direction:

- Dreamlike and psychologically unstable cinema: *All About Lily Chou-Chou*, *Serial Experiments Lain*, *In the Mood for Love*, and *Perfect Blue*.
- Intimate nostalgia and coming-of-age observation: Richard Linklater's work.
- Atmospheric, textured, melancholic, and sometimes distorted music: Tame Impala, Radiohead, Puma Blue, Deftones, and Frank Ocean.
- Album imagery connected to isolation, memory, place, and youth: *Lonerism*, *American Football*, *Shinjuku Mad*, and Joji's artwork.
- Architectural tension between brutalism, Bauhaus order, Art Deco ornament, and old European environments.

## Emerging design principle

Combine emotional, dreamlike atmosphere with disciplined architectural structure and electronic signal language. The contrast should feel culturally specific to Fernando rather than generically “creative” or “futuristic.”

## Mismatches to avoid

- Conventional SaaS landing-page aesthetics.
- Generic technology gradients and decorative dashboard imagery.
- A centered slogan followed by interchangeable feature cards.
- Startup language unsupported by real products or results.
- A sterile developer résumé with no cultural personality.
- Avant-garde gestures that obstruct basic comprehension.
- Green and off-white nature styling used as the portfolio's dominant identity.
- A literal imitation of vintage audio hardware or a fake music player.
