## Running it

```
npm run prototype     # http://localhost:5174/?title=unifraktur
```

Drag empty space to move the camera; drag the Unit to operate it. HUD dials tune the Plate
engraving, the relief, the plate art framing, the camera, and the Vigil.

`window.__unit` is a workbench hook — `pads()`, `parts()`, `screenOf(mesh)`, `render()`, `cam`,
`setCam()`, `setEng()`, `setTitle()`. It exists because rAF is throttled in a background tab, so the
last painted frame's matrices cannot be trusted. All four control paths were verified in a real
browser on 2026-08-25; they had never been clicked before.

## Known rough edges in the prototype

- The lancet window **reads rectangular** — the arch sits above frame at most camera angles.
- **Only the right curtain shows**; the left one's rotation is wrong.
- **No shadows anywhere.** Biggest single realism win available; things sit flat on the desk.
- No painted vault, pictures or chandelier — the reference study has all three.
- Warm candlelight **tints the plate art brown**. Watch this when his artwork lands.
- Geometry is hand-written primitives. It holds to ~74° off vertical, not beyond; the camera is
  clamped for exactly that reason.
- Contact email is hard-coded in `scene.js`. It is his public contact, but treat it as PII.

## One structural thing nobody has decided

**The Vigil is doing two jobs**: candles going out one by one (the rite, interior) and day turning to
night (the sky, exterior). They currently move together on one control. It reads fine now, but
splitting them later — dusk with candles lit, or a dark room in daylight — gets expensive if it goes
unnoticed.
